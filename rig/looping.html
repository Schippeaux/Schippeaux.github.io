Looping

How to loop drums, bass, and synth layers so you can play other instruments and build up a piece.

Method: Session View clip recording

Ableton's Session View is the looper. Fixed-length recording captures a clip of exactly N bars and starts looping it without stopping playback.

Every voice can be looped independently. Audio loops of the drums and bass free your hands for the synth. Audio loops of the MiniFreak free it for new layers — the synth can be re-patched while its last phrase keeps playing. Drums can also be looped as MIDI (the raw zone/velocity data) and re-routed to a different character or voice.

Why loops need the grid

Loops lock to a fixed-length grid, not to each other directly — see Clock for when that grid is set. Without it, independently-played layers drift apart bar by bar: a 4-bar drum loop and a 4-bar bass loop recorded a few milliseconds apart from true tempo will slowly fall out of phase over a long set. Locking every Fixed Length recording to the same frozen grid is what keeps them stacking cleanly instead of smearing.

How to control loops

Hands are on the instrument. Loop control has to come from somewhere else.

Controller options
  • Push 1, first: already owned, already has Fixed Length mode. Clunky for looping because it is a reach from the drums, but free and familiar.
  • Compact MIDI foot controller: Morningstar MC6 (6 switches, programmable), Keith McMillen SoftStep 2 (10 pads, pressure-sensitive), or a simpler 2–4 button unit.
  • Trained SP2 zone as a loop trigger: zero footprint — train a Sensory Percussion zone (e.g. a rim tap or shell hit on a specific drum) to send a MIDI note mapped to Live's record action. The drum is the controller.
  • Small grid controller: Launchpad Mini, APC mini. More visual (LED clip status) but one more thing on the desk.

Note: The Roland Octapad (SPD-30) is a percussion pad instrument, not a loop controller. It plays sounds, not clips.

Example workflow

  1. Set Fixed Length to 4 bars.
  2. Lay down a drum groove. It captures, loops, frees your hands.
  3. Pick up the bass, noodle into a riff. Record a 4-bar bass loop.
  4. Reach for the MiniFreak, play a pad over the top. Record that too.
  5. Now all three are looping. Pick up the sticks again and play live over the bed.
  6. On the turnaround, trigger the next scene — the drum loop evolves, the bass hands off to the synth, the characters wake up.

Open: how loop length is chosen

Loop lengths of 4, 8 and 16 bars cover most of what this rig needs, but which one is live for a given recording is still an open design question. Two shapes are on the table:

  • Three length buttons — dedicated 4/8/16 triggers, one press picks the length and arms the record.
  • One record button plus a length selector — a single transport action, with the length set separately (a knob position, a pad, a mode toggle) before you commit.

Either shape wants pads or knobs within easy hand-reach — Push 1's grid or the modular FX-knob controller (see MIDI Controllers) — rather than a footswitch, which has too few discrete positions to spare for this on top of record/stop/scene/undo.
