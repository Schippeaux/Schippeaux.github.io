Expression

What the playing can do to the synth and effects — from simple and legible to poetic.

Tier 1 — Direct, one to one

Velocity → amplitude of the synth voice · Velocity → filter cutoff: harder is brighter · Zone → patch or layer: edge is a pluck, centre a pad, rim a stab · Speed → cutoff or resonance, continuously · Strike type → separate voice: rimshot stabs, open hits swell

Tier 2 — Shaping rather than switching

Velocity → envelope attack/release: hard is snappy, soft is slow · Position → pan, mirroring where the stick lands · Speed → delay feedback: busy playing builds wash · Speed → LFO rate on a pad's tremolo · Velocity → reverb send: loud blooms, soft stays dry

Tier 3 — Derived and relational

Mapping detailsMax for Live
  • Micro-timing (ahead/behind the grid) → delay time or LFO phase, so rushing or dragging destabilises the texture
  • Dynamic variance → randomisation amount on the generative sequencer: erratic playing makes the system wilder, steady playing calms it
  • Rolling average velocity → intensity macro that blends in unison or extra oscillator layers as energy builds
  • Position drift across a phrase → wavetable blend, giving a phrase an arc rather than a per-hit reaction

Tier 4 — The system has a personality

Mapping detailsMax for Live
  • Silence → growth: an ambient layer swells during rests and recedes on re-entry; stopping becomes a compositional act
  • Call and response: sample the last phrase and answer it a bar later, decayed or reharmonised
  • Fatigue: cumulative density over the set slowly darkens or saturates the tone, giving the whole performance a long arc
  • Decaying influence: each hit nudges a parameter and the nudge decays over seconds, leaving a trail like ripples
  • Surprise threshold: unusually steady playing triggers a rare one-off event — a key change, a drop-out, a new voice
  • Cross-modulation between limbs: the time gap between hat and snare hits drives something neither does alone
  • Harmonic memory: recent choices bias what the sequencer may play next, so the piece remembers where it has been

Suggested order after the first experiment: position → timbre blend (expression independent of volume), then dynamic variance → randomisation amount (the first mapping that feels alive rather than reactive).
