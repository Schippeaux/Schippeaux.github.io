Why footswitches, not just Push

Push 1 already does clips and scenes, but it is a reach from the kit and needs a hand. The Morningstar and the expression pedal move loop transport and continuous control to the feet, freeing both hands for the instrument in front of you.

The hi-hat pedal is a controller as much as it is percussion: its continuous position is read as a CC, the same signalling a footswitch uses, just analogue instead of binary.

Open: where loop-length control lives

Loop lengths of 4, 8 and 16 bars are still an open design question — see Looping. The two shapes under consideration both want pads, not a footswitch: either three dedicated length buttons, or one record button plus a length selector. Push's pads are the natural home, with the modular FX-knob controller (once it arrives) as the other hand-reach option.

The deferred modular controller

An Intech Grid or Monogram-style device: a grid of knobs/pads whose assignment follows the selected track in Live, rather than being hard-wired to one effect. Deferred until the core rig (drums, bass, synth, foot transport) is in and the effects worth dedicating knobs to are known.

Natural jobs once it arrives: the loop-length selector above, and direct hands-on access to whichever Expression mapping (Expression) is live in a given scene — filter cutoff, reverb send, randomisation amount — without diving into Live's screen.
