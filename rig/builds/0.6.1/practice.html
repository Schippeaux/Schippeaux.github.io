How to read the notation

One letter per note. Each group is one beat.

  • R and L are right and left hand. Capital letters are accents, lowercase are normal notes.
  • . is a rest.
  • (l)R is a flam: a quiet left grace note just before an accented right. (ll)R is a drag, with two grace notes. In the step grids the grace notes are the small letters above the main note.
  • |: ... :| means repeat. Four letters per beat is 16th notes. Patterns marked "triplets" have three per beat.

Example: Rlrr Lrll is a single paradiddle, and the capitals show the accent landing on the lead hand.

Swing delays every second 16th note: 50% is straight, about 67% is a triplet feel, 75% is very late. Leave it at 50% while learning the sticking, then use it to hear the same pattern in a jazz feel. It affects playback here only. The downloaded .mid files are straight; in Live you add swing by dropping a groove from the Groove Pool onto the clip.

The sounds are synthesised clicks so you can check timing and sticking. The right hand is always a little higher in pitch than the left. The metronome is a softer, lower beep so it doesn't get confused with your hands.

In the .mid files the right hand is note 38 and the left is note 37, so a Drum Rack can give each hand its own pad, or the same sound. Accents are velocity 112, normal notes 72 and grace notes 50. Grace notes sit a few milliseconds before the beat, and a flam on the first note wraps to the end of the loop. Each file is a whole number of bars: shorter patterns repeat to fill them, so the clip loops cleanly.

A session

  1. Warm up, 3 minutes. Single stroke roll at a tempo that feels easy.
  2. One new pattern, 10 minutes. Slow, with the metronome, until three passes in a row are even.
  3. One review pattern, 5 minutes. Something from last session, a little faster.
  4. Play, 2 minutes. No metronome. Mix anything you know.

A common rule is to raise the tempo by about 4 BPM only after a few clean passes. The aim is the same pattern, repeated until your hands do it without you.

In the Ableton set these loops would sit in a practice scene, played by the stand-in Drum Rack. Once the hands are there, synth layers and effects are added on top and the loops become the environments from the Set Template.
