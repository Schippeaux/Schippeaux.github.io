How to read the notation

Sixteen letters make one bar. Each group of four is one beat.

  • R and L are right and left hand. Capital letters are accents, lowercase are normal notes.
  • . is a rest.
  • (l)R is a flam: a quiet left grace note just before an accented right.
  • |: ... :| means repeat. Patterns marked as triplets have three letters per beat.

Example: Rlrr Lrll is a single paradiddle, and the capitals show the accent landing on the lead hand.

Swing delays every second 16th note: 50% is straight, about 67% is a triplet feel, 75% is very late. Leave it at 50% while learning the sticking, then use it to hear the same pattern in a jazz feel. It affects playback here only. The downloaded .mid files are straight; in Live you add swing by dropping a groove from the Groove Pool onto the clip.

The sound is a simple synthesised snare so you can check timing and sticking. The right hand sits a little higher in pitch than the left to make the sticking audible. In the .mid files both hands use the same snare note (General MIDI 38), so any Drum Rack snare will play them. Accents are velocity 112, normal notes 72.

A session

  1. Warm up, 3 minutes. Single stroke roll at a tempo that feels easy.
  2. One new pattern, 10 minutes. Slow, with the click, until three passes in a row are even.
  3. One review pattern, 5 minutes. Something from last session, a little faster.
  4. Play, 2 minutes. No click. Mix anything you know.

A common rule is to raise the tempo by about 4 BPM only after a few clean passes. The aim is the same pattern, repeated until your hands do it without you.

In the Ableton set these loops would sit in a practice scene, played by the stand-in Drum Rack. Once the hands are there, synth layers and effects are added on top and the loops become the environments from the Set Template.
