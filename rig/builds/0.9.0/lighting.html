Lighting

LEDs behind each mesh head, diffused into a glow and driven off the same signals already feeding the synth — one more device on the existing hub, not a separate system.

Behind the mesh

A single LED behind a mesh head gives a hot spot, not a glow. The plan is a small ring of addressable RGB LEDs (WS2812B / NeoPixel) inside each shell, facing the underside of the head, with a diffuser between them and the mesh — tracing paper, a disc of frosted acrylic, or a layer of white fabric — to spread the light into an even wash. Addressable means every head can be its own segment of one LED string, each independently colourable, rather than needing a separate controller per drum.

One controller, wired into the hub

A single microcontroller drives every strip — a Teensy or an Arduino Pro Micro, both of which present as a real class-compliant USB-MIDI device with no extra drivers, running the FastLED library to push the pixels. It plugs into the same powered USB hub as everything else on Hook-up — lighting is just another device on that hub, not a parallel system with its own cabling.

Why wired MIDI, not WiFi/OSC

OSC over WiFi would carry richer payloads than MIDI's 7-bit range — full RGB and a scene index in one packet rather than a note plus a couple of CCs. But a live set is the wrong place to add a wireless dependency when a USB cable into the hub already does the job. MIDI's 7-bit resolution is plenty for brightness and hue; a wired connection is the one that won't drop out mid-song.

Scenes drive programs

Lighting is treated as another mapped destination in the same signal bus the Chemistry routing and Expression mappings already use — velocity and energy that move a filter cutoff or a reverb send can just as easily move a brightness or a hue.

A handful of MIDI notes select the running program on the microcontroller — idle breathing glow, a flash per hit scaled by velocity, a position-based chase, a colour wash per scene. A couple of CCs carry continuous brightness/hue, fed from the same signals already driving the synth. A small Max for Live device watches for Live scene launches and fires the matching program-select note automatically, so the lighting always tracks whichever environment is playing without a separate manual switch.
