CC numbers are left open on purpose and get assigned in the next pass. Notes and CCs travel together over one virtual MIDI bus.

Why a virtual busIAC

Ableton sums all MIDI coming out of the SP2 plugin onto a single channel, and CCs from the plugin cannot directly drive Live's own knobs. Sunhouse's own examples send MIDI out of Sensory Percussion over the IAC Bus Driver and back into Live. Building the stand-in the same way means the real kit is a re-point, not a rewrite.

The SP2 plugin also needs Live 11.3 or later. See Percussion for the controller types.

Tracks

Three synth slots named by position, not by role. The scene decides whether a slot plays bass, keys, pad or stabs.

TrackHoldsNotes

Each slot is an Instrument Rack with one chain per environment. In the prototype phase the MiniFreak's audio comes in through its own USB interface; later it goes through the Portal. See Synth.

Chains

Switching chains per sceneLive 11

Put a clip in the scene on that slot, with a clip envelope on the rack's Chain Selector. Launching the scene moves the selector. If that does not work in Live 11, the fallback is a mapped button or a Push 1 control.

Turning unused chains fully offCPU

Yes, with some setup. Ableton says racks already conserve CPU in inactive chains. Its method for going further is to map each device's on/off to the Chain Selector, so only the selected chain has an active device.

  • Wrap each chain's contents in one nested rack, so there is one on/off per chain instead of one per device.
  • Map that wrapper's on/off to the Chain Selector range for its chain.
  • Watch Live's CPU meter before and after. Stock devices are cheap, so the saving may be small; heavy plugins gain most.

Turning a device off also cuts its tail. The MiniFreak chain costs no computer CPU either way, because it is hardware.

Looping

Drums and synths sum into a Play bus. Loop 1 and Loop 2 record from it, before the returns, so a loop captures what was played and not the room around it.

A scene with no clip on a loop track leaves that track alone, so its loop carries over. A scene with a clip or a stop button replaces it. Push 1 handles record, overdub and undo for now; see Looping for the controller plan.

Tempo and carried loops

A scene that carries a loop has to keep the tempo, or the warped loop retimes. In the prototype, tempo is typed per scene. With the real kit, the rule on Clock applies instead: follow the playing, then freeze at the first loop.

MiniFreak recall

Each scene's MiniFreak clip carries a Bank and a Program, so launching the scene recalls the patch. Arturia's chart gives this layout.

PresetsBankProgram change
1 to 128Bank 10 to 127
129 to 256Bank 20 to 127
257 and aboveBank 30 to 127

Keep the environment patches together in one bank so recall is predictable, for example environments 1 to 4 as presets 1 to 4.

Not yet confirmed

Which bank-select message the MiniFreak listens to. A user report says Bank 2 is value 1 and Bank 3 is value 2, but that is not the manual. Arturia's forum points to page 110 of the hardware manual. Test by launching a clip with a Bank and Program and watching the MiniFreak display, and check for an off-by-one, since Live may number programs from 1.

Build order

Each step ends with something finished. This is a real sequence, so the numbers mean something. The last step is the signal to move on to the drums.

  1. Wiring check. The stand-in kit sends one CC over IAC Bus 1 and one mapped knob moves. Nothing else.
  2. One environment. A MiniFreak patch, one chain, one effect chain, the drum loop playing. Bounce it.
  3. One clean loop. One layer and one overdub that hold together. Bounce it.
  4. One reactive environment. The Timbre lane moving a destination. Bounce it.
  5. Three or four environments as scenes, each bounced and named.
