About these

Written in the spirit of Arthur Hnatek, Jojo Mayer, Max Cooper, Floating Points and Tipper. They are original study patterns, not transcriptions of any recording.

Each groove has a different feel, and that feel is part of the MIDI file: some swing the 16th notes, and some drums sit a little late or early. Because it is baked into the notes, there is no global swing control. The tempo slider on each card changes the speed only. The .mid file keeps the groove's own tempo.

I can't hear these. I wrote them from how these styles are generally built, so treat them as first drafts to react to. Tell me which ones feel right, which feel wrong, and what to change.

  • X accent, x normal, o ghost (quiet), a blank cell is a rest. One cell is a 16th note.
  • Rows are drums, top to bottom from cymbals to kick. A heavier line marks each beat group.
  • The page plays a simple synthesised kit so you can check the rhythm. The .mid files are meant for a real Drum Rack.

The notes follow General MIDI, so they land on the standard Drum Rack pads. Velocity is 122 to 32 depending on the drum and the mark (X, x or o). Beat 1 is tick 0, and there are 480 ticks to a beat. A hit that lands a little before the downbeat in the first bar wraps to the end of the loop. The 7/8 groove has a 7/8 time signature in its file, so Live will show the clip in 7/8. The three-bar groove needs a three-bar clip.
